--- title: "Calibrating a rainfall-runoff simulation" order: 3 venue: "Journal of Statistical Theory and Practice · 2023" org: "With Pritam Ranjan and Abhyuday Mandal · Journal of Statistical Theory and Practice (2023)" description: "Gaussian-process surrogate models with sequential experimental design to calibrate an expensive black-box simulator." image: /assets/projects/jstp-fit.png links: - label: "Paper (DOI)" url: "https://doi.org/10.1007/s42519-022-00312-5" ---

Hydrologists model rainfall runoff with a simulator, but nobody knows which input settings make it behave like a real site. Each run is expensive, the output is a series over 5,445 time points, and the observed data is noisier than anything the simulator produces. The task is to find the settings whose output matches reality, in as few runs as possible.

Our approach compresses the target series to a handful of carefully chosen time points using regression splines, then solves the matching problem at each point with Gaussian process surrogates and sequential design. Fifty simulator runs total. My part was the engineering: I implemented the surrogate models and ran the large parallelized simulation studies on an HPC cluster that benchmarked the method against its competitors.

On the real runoff data, from a composting facility in Athens, Georgia, the method beat both established competitors on every goodness-of-fit measure, with roughly 20% lower error than the standard scalarization approach and an R² of 0.93. It also quantifies the uncertainty in the recovered inputs, so you know how far to trust the tuning.
